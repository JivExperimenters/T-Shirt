Executive Summary & Thursday In-Class Sharing Brief
Student & Course: Jivitesh Duddu | IS117 (Intro to Website Development, NJIT) | Date: November 5, 2026
Research Question: "How do competing online T-shirt retailers across novelty graphic, wholesale blank, and custom/blank hybrid models explain value, organize visual hierarchy, and reduce cost or fit uncertainty for a first-time desktop buyer?"
Competitors Audited: 1. Crazy Dog T-Shirts (crazydogtshirts.com — D2C novelty/humor graphic tees)
2. CheapesTees (cheapestees.com — everyday wholesale blank & custom basics)
3. Jiffy (jiffy.com — rapid-delivery blank tees, DTF transfers, and custom print mockup platform)
+ Adjacent UX Reference: Mobbin (On iOS 'Adding to Bag' flow for size-context retention).
Main Analytical Finding: Modernist interface organization (clean grids, upfront delivery dates, and multi-tier size/color pricing tables on Jiffy and CheapesTees) reduces buying friction far better than cluttered overlays, while Postmodernist expressive humor (Crazy Dog) and relatable lifestyle framing build stronger emotional liking. However, hidden cart additions (such as CheapesTees' pre-selected $0.49 Shipping Protection) or steep single-item custom print jumps (Jiffy's $7.53 blank tee rising to $48.33 with custom printing and Saturday rush shipping) create checkout friction.
Primary Recommendation for My T-Shirt Website: Combine a Modernist, distraction-free shopping grid and upfront ZIP-code delivery/cost transparency (adapted from Jiffy and CheapesTees) with aspect-specific customer reviews (fit, fabric weight, print durability) and relatable, humor-driven product collections (adapted from Crazy Dog).

Direction 1: Modernist Interface Grid with Expressive Product Showcases
Observation: CheapesTees [E06] and Jiffy [E10–E11] use clean Modernist grids that make prices and sizes effortless to scan, whereas Crazy Dog [E01] obscures its layout with pop-up wheels.

Direction 2: Aspect-Specific Customer Reviews (Fit, Fabric & Print Quality)
Observation: Jiffy [E10] (9,708 reviews) and CheapesTees [E06] (1,013 reviews) display aggregate star counts, while Crazy Dog [E03] omits fabric weight and fit details above the fold.
Interpretation: Aggregate stars signal popularity (Social Proof), but first-time T-shirt buyers still hesitate over shrinkage, softness, and sizing accuracy.
Recommendation: Display structured customer reviews broken down by specific shirt attributes—Fit (True to Size slider), Fabric Softness/Weight, and After-Wash Print Quality—directly below the size selector.
Tradeoff: Requires structured review prompts and more vertical space on the PDP.

Direction 3: Relatable Copywriting + Upfront Cost & Discount Transparency
Observation: Crazy Dog [E01–E03] builds strong Liking through relatable humor, while Jiffy [E09, E12] builds trust by showing exact ZIP delivery dates and free-shipping thresholds before checkout (unlike CheapesTees' pre-checked $0.49 fee [E08]).
Interpretation: Combining relatable category copy with transparent, opt-in-only cart pricing reduces checkout hesitation.
Recommendation: Pair relatable, community-focused product descriptions (Liking) with an upfront delivery/discount banner (e.g., open 10% student/first-order code and a visible free-shipping calculator in the cart drawer with zero pre-checked add-on fees).
Tradeoff: Foregoes passive revenue from pre-checked shipping insurance.
Validation Test: Track how many test users accurately state their final delivered cost from the cart screen without clicking into the payment form.
